## Major Events in the History of Complex Life

Earth originated 4.5 BYA.  
Life originated 4 BYA.  
Animal fossils appeard 541 MYA.  

Given the apparent productivity of complex life on Earth, why did it take about 3 billion years after the earliest evidence of life for complex, diverse life forms to occur?

### What is complex life?

Multicellular, mobile organisms that interact with each other and their environment.

### Biological diversity and disparity

Diversity is the number of different kinds of organisms. Disparity is the range of different kinds of organisms, usually indicated by phylum-level differences.

The long-term trend in biological diversity is net positive, but there have been many mass extinctions. This chart by Sepkoski (1981) shows the change in diversity over geological time in terms of number of marine families. 

![Biological diversity chart by Sepkoski (1981)](/biol-0210/biological-diversity-sepkoski.png)

### Evolution

Evolution is the change in gene frequencies in a population over time. 
There are 4 mechanisms of evolution: mutation, migration, drift, and selection.
Mutation is the only mechanism that actually introduces novelty.

For natural selection to occur, we make a few assumptions:
1. Traits are heritable
2. Traits impact fitness
3. Traits that positively impact fitness are selected for, and traits that negatively impact fitness are selected against

Speciation happens when there is reduced gene flow between populations, and they become reproductively isolated. Species are usually defined as the largest group of organisms in which two individuals can produce fertile offspring.

### Phylogenies 

Diversity can be explained by lines of descent. **Cladograms** are based on shared traits, while **phylogenies** are cladograms that are specifically based on shared genes. Note that some organisms that are closely related are dissimilar, and vice versa.

![Vertebrate phylogeny](/biol-0210/vertebrate-phylogeny.png)

**Relatedness** is based on the most recent common ancestor. Recency is measured in genealogical time, not calendar time.

A **clade** is a groups of organisms that include an ancestor and all its descendants.

A **monophyletic group** (single tree) is a clade, while a **paraphyletic group** is a group that includes an ancestor but not all its descendants. 

The **crown group** is the last common ancestor of all living members of a clade and all of its descendents. The **pan group** is the crown group and all organisms that share an ancestor with the crown more recently than any other living organism. The **stem group** is the pan group minus the crown group.

For example, crown group Aves is the Neornithes. The pan group Aves is all animals more closely related to Neornithes than to Crocodylia. The stem group Aves is Archaeopteryx.

[Aves phylogeny](/biol-0210/aves-phylogeny.png)

An animal is a monophyletic group of five clades. Genomic data is unclear about whether ctenophores or sponges are the sister group to all other animals.

![Animal phylogeny](/biol-0210/animal-phylogeny.png)

### Ediacaran Period (635-541 MYA)

- No fossils of large organisms were thought to exist until the discovery of *Charnia masoni* in the Charnwood forest (600 MYA) in 1957
- More Ediacarian fossils were found, including *dickinsonia*, *kimberella*, *spriggina*, and *Ikaria wariaootia*
- It was discovered in 2018 that *dickinsonia* was an animal, since the fossils contained cholesteroids and the surrounding sediment does not.
- Some Ediacaran organisms seemed to be bilaterally symmetric.

Question: Why were stem-group bilaterians not found in the pre-Cambrian fossil record?

Hypotheses:

1. They were very small. 
    
    This is unlikely because certain features of bilaterians, like the blood-vascular system, only developed in large organisms. We have also been able to find very small trace fossils before.

2. They were large but have not been located due to rarity. 

    This is unlikely because there is an extensive trace fossil record.

2. They were very simple, so phylum-level distinctions only arose at the Cambrian. 

    Possible, but not possible to verify.

### The Cambrian explosion (541 MYA) 

- First but not only diversification event
- First and only disparitification event 
- Led to the appearance of most phyla (37) and unambiguous animal life
- Defined by the first appearance of the trace fossil *Treptichnus pedum*
- Other fossils include *trilobites*, *anomalocaris* ("anomalous shrimp")

Trilobites, complex compound eyes, exoskeleton, spikes, and lids

- Defines the appearance of complex, visible life in the fossil record
- Marked the appearance of most phyla (37)
- Unambiguous animal life

The existence of the Cambrian explosion is supported by:
1. The reading of the fossil record
2. [Molecular clock analysis](#molecular-clock)

Why did the Cambrian explosion happen?

1. Increase in O2

    Limited O2 could have limited animal evolution, since it is necessary for collagen synthesis. It is generally agreed that animals could not have evolved if O2 was \<1\% present (Twoe limit).

    Raff and Raff (1970) calculated that the maximum tissue thickness at 1\% O2 was 0.2 mm, relying on diffusion. However, Budd and Jensen 2000 found that with a central fluid cavity there is no obvious size limit. The Annelid *Arenicola* can survive with 4\% present.

    The problem with this theory is that substantial O2 had already exceeded the Towe limit long before the Cambrian explosion.

2. Snowball Earth

    The Earth experienced deep freezes during the Cryogenian period and Ediacaran period, and Ediacaran organisms appeared after the last of these events. 

    The problem is that the last deep freeze was *much earlier* than increased disparity in the fossil record. It's not clear how this could have led to increased disparity. 

3. C isotope anomaly

    Isotope anomalies and mass extinctions could lead to diversification. This is analogous to mammal diversification after the KT boundary. However, this is not a fair comparison since the KT event involved the replacement of a few clades by a sister clade. The Cambrian explosion involved an increase in disparity.

4. Adaptive radiation 

    While adaptive radiation could explain changes in diversity (Darwin's finches, Hawaiian honeycreepers), it cannot explain the increase in disparity.

5. Ecosystem engineering
    
    Ecosystem engineering is the modification of the environment by organisms. 

    The first evidence of predation occurred near the end of the Ediacaran, perhaps leading to biomineralized shells and hard skeletons. This could have been facilitated by oxygenation of the atmosphere, leading to the establishment of bottom-up food webs. 

    While this is a necessary condition for the Cambrian explosion, it is not a sufficient condition.


### Molecular clock

Divergence time is how long ago two organisms shared a common ancestor.

The number of genetic differences between two species is proportional to the time since they diverged from a common ancestor. In other words, g(t) = r * t. Finding the rate in this equation requires calibration. 

Using molecular clock, Erwin et al. (2011) measured the divergence times for >100 animals, and mapped those ages onto the animal phylogeny. They concluded that most phylum-level crown-group divergences occurred during the end of the Ediacaran and the Cambrian, which suggests the robustness of the fossil record.

![Erwin et al. (2011)](/biol-0210/erwin-et-al-2011.png)

This is because mutations accumulate at a roughly constant rate over time.
